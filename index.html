How our livestream signal flows, today and proposed

A plain-language walkthrough of how video and graphics currently get from the cameras to the stream and the foyer, and a proposed change that spreads the work more evenly across our equipment.

Before we start

Key terms

A few words come up a lot below. No need to memorize these, just refer back if something's unclear.

ATEM Mini Pro
The video switcher. Takes in all camera feeds and lets one person choose which camera is live.
ProPresenter
The software that displays lyrics, verses, and graphics on our screens.
FOH (Front of House)
The two projector screens the congregation sees.
Confidence Monitor
The on-stage screen showing presenters and worship team the current or next slide.
NDI
A way of sending video over a normal network cable instead of an HDMI cable.
Keying
A technique where the switcher removes a plain background color from a graphic, so only the lyrics show on top of the camera picture.
OBS
Free software that takes a finished video feed and sends it out live to YouTube and Facebook.
USB webcam output
A built-in ATEM feature that lets it act like a plug-in camera for a computer, sending its output directly.
Good news first

What stays exactly the same

No matter which setup we use below, these two paths don't change at all.

Diagram: ProPresenter on Mac 1 sends two separate outputs. The first goes through an HDMI splitter to the F O H screen, feeding Projector 1 and Projector 2, carrying lyrics, graphics, and any video clips ProPresenter plays. The second goes out over DisplayPort, through a DisplayPort to HDMI converter, to the 70 inch confidence monitor mounted right behind the mixing station.

FOH shows only what ProPresenter is presenting, lyrics, graphics, or a video clip it's playing, never the live camera picture. The confidence monitor runs off DisplayPort straight from Mac 1 rather than through the DL7400 dock, since the TV only takes HDMI, that needs a DisplayPort to HDMI converter in between. It sits right behind the mixing station, so it's a short run either way, just long enough to need a proper DisplayPort cable rather than a short default one.

Today

How the broadcast signal flows right now

The camera picture and the lyrics get combined by ProPresenter itself, on the same Mac that runs our presentation. That finished picture is then sent over our network to a second Mac, which streams it out.

Diagram: Cameras 1 to 3 connect to the ATEM Mini Pro, which switches between them. The ATEM connects to Mac 1 running ProPresenter, which receives the camera video and adds lyrics on top of it. Mac 1 sends that combined picture over the network, using NDI, to Mac 2 running OBS. Mac 2 sends the finished stream to YouTube and Facebook.

Worth knowing

  • Mac 1 is doing several jobs at once: receiving live camera video, combining it with lyrics, running FOH and the confidence monitor, and sending the result over the network.
  • If Mac 1 or ProPresenter has a hiccup, the stream and screens are all affected at the same time, since they all pass through the same computer.
  • Mac 2 also runs Lightkey, our lighting control software, alongside OBS.
Proposed

How it would work instead

The ATEM combines the camera picture and the lyrics itself, using a technique called keying. ProPresenter only has to prepare the lyrics graphic and hand it to the ATEM, it never touches live camera video. The ATEM then sends the one finished picture straight out to two places at once.

Diagram: Cameras 1 to 3, and Mac 1 running ProPresenter sending only the lyrics graphic, both feed into the ATEM Mini Pro. The ATEM keys the lyrics onto the camera picture, producing one finished programme. That finished programme splits into two independent paths: by USB directly to Mac 2 running OBS, which sends the stream to YouTube and Facebook, and by HDMI directly to the foyer display.

Why this is an improvement

  • Each device only does one job: ATEM handles video, ProPresenter handles graphics.
  • If ProPresenter has a problem, the stream and foyer keep running, we'd only lose the lyrics overlay briefly.
  • One fewer step between camera and stream, generally a cleaner, faster picture.
  • Mac 2 still gives full control to cut to a backup screen if anything goes wrong, exactly as it does today.
  • Foyer gets the real programme (camera and graphics together), directly from the ATEM.

On the projectors specifically

  • Both FOH projectors take HDMI, not USB-C. Church install projectors are almost always HDMI-only, USB-C video input is mainly a laptop/monitor feature, not something projectors typically have. Worth a quick look at the back of each unit to confirm before buying the splitter, but this is a safe assumption.
  • The splitter sits between the DL7400's HDMI output and the two projectors, one HDMI in, two HDMI out.

Getting there

What it takes to switch

  1. Buy a basic HDMI splitter for the two FOH projectors (roughly £15–25), if we don't already have one.
  2. Buy a DisplayPort to HDMI converter and a DisplayPort cable long enough to reach the confidence monitor, since the TV only accepts HDMI and we're running this straight from Mac 1's own DisplayPort output.
  3. Set up "keying" on the ATEM once: this is a one-time configuration where we tell it to remove the plain background behind our lyrics graphic, so only the text shows over the camera picture.
  4. Connect the ATEM's second output directly to the foyer display.
  5. Test the whole chain during a rehearsal or a lower-stakes midweek service before relying on it for a main service.

One thing to test carefully

  • Keying can look slightly less crisp around text edges than what ProPresenter does today, worth checking with our actual fonts and colors before committing to the switch.
  • If it doesn't look right after testing, our current setup remains a completely valid fallback, nothing is lost by trying this gradually.
